How Drying Works on a Lancaster Water Damage Job

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Lancaster property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most Lancaster homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. The equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not when the calendar does.

What to Do Before Our Lancaster Crew Arrives

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. These are the mistakes our crews see most often in Lancaster, and what to do instead.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Water Damage We See Most in Lancaster

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Lancaster, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Lancaster properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.

Water Damage Insurance Claims in Lancaster

Adjusters work from evidence, so the strongest thing a Lancaster homeowner can bring to a water damage claim is a good record. Start building it before anything is cleaned up or thrown away.

Get pictures of whatever failed, whether a hose, a valve or a pipe joint, and hang on to the part itself. Take wide shots of each room and close shots of damaged items, including appliance labels and serial numbers. Hold damaged flooring and possessions until your adjuster has inspected them or said that pictures are enough. Keep receipts for anything you spend because of the loss, from fans to a hotel stay.

Many policies treat an abrupt failure differently from long-term seepage or flood water coming in from outdoors. How your policy treats each is a question for your agent or adjuster, and we will not guess at it for you.
