Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Harrisburg: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. We serve Harrisburg homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. This is where our team comes in. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the Water SupplyClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
  • A P-trap or drain connection under a sink that loosens and drips each time the basin empties.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Damaged Flooring

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Rotted Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Once framing lumber is no longer sound, it must be removed and new wood put in.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • How much drywall, insulation, cabinetry or flooring has to be removed and replaced.
  • Whether the damage stayed in one room or spread to others and to the level below.
  • Any mold found, since it must be contained and removed before the drying can be completed.
  • Which finishes and flooring got wet, as some can be saved by drying and some cannot.
